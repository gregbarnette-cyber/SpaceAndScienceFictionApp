Hyper Limits as dotted rings — four ways to fit all 44

Follow-ups to Option C. All keep the star-at-centre, dotted-ring, √AU idiom of the Ice Line / System Regions maps and the app's spectral palette. Nothing in the app was changed.

The density problem, measured. The 44 limits span 0.68–5.96 AU, but 40 of them (F0–M9) are packed into 1.11–3.18 AU. On a √AU dial with a 230 px radius that's ~67 px of radius for 40 rings — 1.7 px apart, thinner than the stroke. So "draw all 44 as full circles" is not achievable at any scale; each variant below trades something specific to get there.

V1 — Ghost rings + class ringsall 44 drawn, 8 labelled

Every subtype is drawn as a very faint dotted ring; on top, each class's hottest and coolest ring is drawn bold and labelled once (G · 2.65–2.17 AU). You see the true texture — where the catalogue is dense, where it jumps — without 44 labels. Hover any ghost ring for its exact subtype. This is the smallest departure from your Option C.

Good

  • Literally shows all 44 rings.
  • Only 8 labels — no collisions.
  • Closest to the ice-line chart already in the app.

Costs

  • Ghost rings merge into a haze in the F–M band.
  • Individual subtypes are hover-only.

V2 — Class sectorsall 44 readable, zero collisions

The dial is divided into eight 45° wedges, one per class. Each subtype is drawn as an arc at its true radius, but only inside its own class's wedge — so the F arcs and the G arcs never overlap even though their radii nearly do. Class name sits at the wedge edge, and each wedge's first/last subtype is labelled along its mid-angle. All 44 values are individually visible and pickable, at true distance.

Good

  • Every subtype is a distinct, hittable mark.
  • Radius stays honest — an arc's distance from centre is its real AU.
  • Grouping by class is structural, not just colour.

Costs

  • Angle carries no meaning — needs one line of caption to say so.
  • Novel form; not currently in the app anywhere.

V3 — Class rings with drill-down8 by default, 10 on demand

Default view is 8 clean labelled class rings (the class's midpoint, with its span shaded). Click a class — or its legend chip — and that class expands into its full set of subtype rings, each labelled, while the other classes fade to thin guides. Click again to collapse. Same interaction the OEC architecture map already uses (click a node to recenter).

Good

  • Uncluttered at rest; full detail one click away.
  • Labels are always legible — never more than 10 at once.
  • Matches an interaction pattern already in the codebase.

Costs

  • The whole catalogue is never on screen at once.
  • A static PNG export (e.g. the dossier) shows only the collapsed state.

V4 — Filtered ringslegend-filter idiom (O16)

All subtype rings of the selected classes are drawn full and labelled; unchecked classes vanish. Start with two or three classes ticked. This reuses the per-class legend filter the star charts already have (Phase O O16), so it needs no new interaction vocabulary — the legend is the control. A zoom toggle ("inner focus / full range", as on the ice-line chart) handles the fact that O sits twice as far out as M9.

Good

  • Zero new UI concepts — it's the existing legend filter.
  • Every visible ring is fully labelled at its true radius.
  • Auto-fit turns a crowded band into a readable one.

Costs

  • Default state has to pick classes for the user.
  • Ticking all 8 reproduces the hairball — needs a cap or an auto-collapse.

If you want one: V2 (class sectors) is the only variant where all 44 are simultaneously visible, individually labelled, and at true radius — it answers "show them all, grouped by spectral type" exactly. V1 is the safest and cheapest if you'd rather stay inside the existing ice-line chart's shape. V3 and V4 are the same chart plus an interaction, and they compose — V3's drill-down could sit on top of V1's ghost rings.
