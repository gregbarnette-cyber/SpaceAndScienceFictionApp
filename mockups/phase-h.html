Roche Limit Calculator

Rigid-body and fluid Roche limits for a satellite orbiting a primary body (planet–moon or star–planet). Inside the Roche limit, tidal forces tear a satellite apart.

Plan details
  • compute_roche_limit(primary_mass_earth, satellite_density_gcc, primary_radius_earth=None)
  • Rigid: R × 1.26 × (ρ_primary/ρ_sat)^(1/3) · Fluid: R × 2.456 × (…)
  • Radius estimated from mass (R = 6371·M^0.55 km) if left blank.
  • Corrected: rigid coeff 1.26 (not 2.44). Verified Earth–Moon ≈ 9,490 / 18,490 km.

Tidal Locking Timescale

Estimates how long a satellite takes to become tidally locked to its primary (MacDonald 1964 torque model). An order-of-magnitude worldbuilding estimate.

Plan details
  • compute_tidal_locking_time(primary_mass_earth, satellite_mass_earth, sma_km, initial_rotation_hours, rigidity_pa=3e10, tidal_q=100)
  • T = (ω₀·a⁶·I·Q) / (3·G·M_primary²·k₂·R_sat⁵), I=0.4·M·R², k₂≈0.3
  • Strong a⁶ dependence — small SMA changes move the answer a lot.

Hill Sphere Calculator

The gravitational sphere of influence of a planet within a star system. Stable satellite orbits exist within ~0.5 × the Hill radius.

Plan details
  • compute_hill_sphere(star_mass_solar, planet_mass_earth, sma_au, eccentricity=0)
  • r_H = a·(1−e)·(M_planet/(3·M_star))^(1/3) · stable limit = 0.5·r_H
  • Earth check: ≈ 1,496,000 km (0.0100 AU); the Moon at 384,400 km is well inside.

Binary Star Orbit Stability

Is a planet's orbit dynamically stable in a binary star system? Holman & Wiegert (1999) empirical fit. S-type = planet orbits one star; P-type = planet orbits both (circumbinary).

Plan details
  • compute_binary_orbit_stability(mass1_solar, mass2_solar, binary_sma_au, test_sma_au, eccentricity=0)
  • S-type stable if test SMA < S-critical; P-type stable if test SMA > P-critical.
  • Corrected: P-type uses +4.12μ (not −4.12μ, which goes negative). μ=0.5,e=0 → S≈0.274·a_b, P≈2.388·a_b.

Atmosphere Retention Calculator

Which atmospheric gases can a planet retain against Jeans escape, given its mass, radius, and equilibrium temperature?

Plan details
  • compute_atmosphere_retention(planet_mass_earth, planet_radius_earth, temperature_k)
  • Jeans λ = (G·M·m_gas)/(k_B·T·R). λ>6 Retained · 3–6 Escaping slowly · ≤3 Lost rapidly.
  • Uses equilibrium temperature → optimistic about retention (a worldbuilding heuristic, not an exospheric-escape simulation).
In the app, the Status column is color-coded: Retained · Escaping slowly · Lost rapidly.
